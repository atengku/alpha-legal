Data Dictionary and Provenance Standard
Standard SAL-DDF v1.0  ·  Effective June 2026  ·  Companion to the Product Brief

Every value the platform publishes is classified by how it is produced, from primary-source fact to standardized analytic. The point of this framework is transparency of provenance: a buyer can see exactly which fields are republished verbatim from the court record, which are counts, which are interpreted, and which are the proprietary analytics that have been defined to a fixed standard. The layer of an element sets its evidentiary weight and its licensing terms.

The five data layers
Real, republished
14 fields
Lifted verbatim from the judgment or its public record. Highest evidentiary weight.
Aggregated
7 fields
Counts and rollups computed by grouping real data. Deterministic.
Inferred
9 fields
Classified by heuristic from the text. Interpretive, with stated rules.
Analytics & correlation
10 fields
Standardized proprietary metrics and indices. The sought-after layer.
Counts & wins
5 fields
The litigation tally primitives that feed every win metric.

Why the standard matters

The analytics layer is where the value sits, and its worth depends on the definitions being fixed. Each metric here is computed the same way for every judge, firm, and lawyer, on the same clean-outcome rule, the same minimum sample thresholds, and the same significance weights. That is what makes a win rate or an MVP index comparable across the corpus rather than an anecdote. Real and aggregated layers carry the highest evidentiary weight; inferred and analytic layers are standardized but interpretive, and are licensed and cited accordingly. Win rates and indices reflect case selection and case strength, not skill or judicial favour, and some segments rest on small samples.
