Litigant-in-person · Civil · Singapore

File a civil suit yourself, without getting lost.

An end-to-end map of self-representation in the Singapore civil courts: from working out which forum can hear you, through filing and serving, contesting, trial, and finally getting paid. Built as the product spine for a guided self-filing app.

Reality check, not legal advice. This framework explains court procedure so a person can navigate the mechanics. It is not legal advice and does not tell you whether your case is strong, what to argue, or how to draft your pleadings. The Service Bureau and the courts cannot give legal advice either. For the merits of a dispute, free options exist (Community Justice Centre, Legal Aid Bureau, CLAS, Law Society Pro Bono). Procedure changes; figures and form numbers must be re-verified against the live court sources listed at the end before any build ships.
01

Triage: which door do I walk through?

The forum is decided mostly by what you are claiming and how much. Answer three questions; the navigator routes you to the right forum and the right filing system. This is the app's front door.

    02

    The forums, and the two filing systems

    Two completely different online systems sit behind self-filing. Tribunals use CJTS, which a self-represented person logs into directly. The civil courts use eLitigation, which only law firms can access online; a self-represented person must file through a physical Service Bureau.

    Small Claims Tribunals (SCT)

    ≤ S$20,000 (≤ S$30,000 by mutual consent)

    Consumer-supplier disputes: sale of goods, provision of services, residential tenancy ≤ 2 years, certain property damage, unfair practices under the CPFTA. No lawyers allowed. Two-year limitation.

    Files via CJTSNo lawyersSelf-serve online

    Employment Claims Tribunals (ECT)

    ≤ S$20,000 (≤ S$30,000 if union/TADM mediated)

    Statutory salary and employment-related money claims. Mandatory mediation at TADM first, which produces the certificate you need to file.

    Files via CJTSTADM first

    Community Disputes Resolution Tribunals (CDRT)

    Neighbour disputes

    Disputes between neighbours over unreasonable interference (noise, smell, obstruction). Pre-filing requirements and a strong steer toward mediation apply.

    Files via CJTSMediation steer

    Protection from Harassment Court (PHC)

    Harassment / false statements

    Protection orders and related relief under POHA. Simplified proceedings are filed through CJTS; other PHC matters route differently.

    Files via CJTS

    Magistrate's Court (MC)

    ≤ S$60,000

    General civil claims at the lower band. The simplified civil process (Order 65) applies by default: upfront document production, limited interlocutory applications, simplified case conferences.

    Files via eLitigationService BureauOrder 65 default

    District Court (DC)

    S$60,001 – S$250,000 (PI/road-traffic to S$500,000)

    The workhorse civil court. Above the limit only by the parties' consent. Simplified process applies if all parties consent.

    Files via eLitigationService Bureau

    General Division, High Court

    Above S$250,000 · unlimited

    High-value and complex matters, and appeals from the State Courts. Self-representation is permitted but the procedural and evidential demands are heaviest here.

    Files via eLitigationService Bureau

    Specialist branches scope stub

    Out of scope for v0.1

    Probate, insolvency and bankruptcy, family, admiralty, judicial review, IP, SICC, and company-law applications follow their own rules and forms. Flagged here so the app routes users out to the correct specialist track rather than mis-filing.

    03

    The workflows, step by step

    Pick a track. Each shows the sequence, the form at each step, the statutory clock, the decision branches, and the honest friction points. Every track closes with three outcome scenarios: best, likely, and worst case.

    Small Claims Tribunals

    The cheapest, fastest civil route for the right kind of dispute. You file and manage everything yourself in CJTS, no lawyer permitted, and the process is built around a consultation (mediation) before any hearing.

    Gate first: only specific subject matter qualifies. Run the CJTS pre-filing assessment; if it fails, the dispute belongs in a normal court instead.

    Service

    1. Confirm eligibility
      Check the subject matter qualifies (goods, services, residential tenancy ≤ 2 years, non-vehicle property damage, CPFTA unfair practice) and that you are within 2 years of the cause of action.
      CJTS pre-filing assessmentFail → use a normal court
    2. Try eNegotiation / eMediation (optional)
      CJTS lets you attempt online resolution with the other side before committing to a claim. Cheap and often faster than a hearing.
      CJTS eNegotiation
    3. File the Claim
      Log in to CJTS, open the Claim Form, enter your pre-filing assessment ID and the facts, attach evidence, choose a consultation date, and pay. The claim is only processed once paid.
      Claim Form (CJTS)Filing fee (value-scaled)
    4. Serve the claim and Notice of Consultation
      Print the claim and the Notice of Consultation and serve the respondent within 7 days, by an allowed method. Then file a Declaration of Service with proof.
      Serve within 7 daysDeclaration of Service (CJTS)
      Friction pointServing correctly is where most self-litigants slip. If you cannot serve, attend the consultation anyway; the registrar can direct substituted service (for example, by email).
    5. Attend the consultation (mediation)
      A registrar mediates. Many cases settle here and end with a consent order. Bring all evidence and a clear number.
      Registrar-led
      BranchSettle → consent order, matter closes. No settlement → proceeds to a tribunal hearing.
    6. Hearing before a tribunal magistrate
      Private hearing, both sides present case and evidence in turn. The magistrate decides on the merits and the law and issues an Order of Tribunal.
      Order of Tribunal
    7. If unpaid: enforce
      An SCT money order is enforced through the normal court enforcement machinery (see the Enforcement module), not by the tribunal itself.
      → Enforcement module
    Bull · best case

    Eligible claim, clean documents, respondent settles at consultation with a consent order paid on the spot.

    Time: 4–8 weeks   Cost: filing fee only
    Base · likely

    No settlement at consultation, a short hearing follows, you obtain an order, respondent pays within the deadline.

    Time: 2–4 months   Cost: low
    Bear · worst case

    Respondent ignores the order. You must open separate enforcement proceedings, locate assets, and may still recover little if there is nothing to seize.

    Time: 6+ months   Cost: fees, low recovery

    A purpose-built forum for neighbour-versus-neighbour disputes about unreasonable interference with enjoyment of a residence. The system pushes hard toward mediation first, and a court order here is a last resort.

    Protection from Harassment Court

    For protection orders and related relief under the Protection from Harassment Act. Simplified proceedings (the common self-filed route) go through CJTS. Where the matter is urgent or involves serious threats, expedited orders may be sought.

      Protection Order (PO), Expedited Protection Order (EPO) where there is urgency/risk, or relief for false statements. Gather evidence of the conduct (messages, recordings, dates).
      PO / EPO / false-statement relief

      Complete the application setting out the harassing conduct and the order sought. Pay any fee.
      PHC application (CJTS)
    3. Serve the respondent
      Serve as directed and file proof. For an EPO the court may act before full service given urgency.
      Declaration of Service

      The court hears the matter and may grant the protection order with conditions. Breach of a PO carries consequences.
      Protection Order
    Bull

    Strong evidence, EPO granted quickly, conduct stops.

    Base

    PO granted after a hearing; respondent complies.

    Bear

    Evidence thin or respondent contests hard; order refused or repeatedly breached, requiring further action.

    Originating Claim · the general civil lawsuit

    This is the main event: the route when the material facts are in dispute (formerly the "Writ of Summons"). It runs in the Magistrate's Court, District Court, or General Division of the High Court depending on value. A self-represented person must file every document through eLitigation at a LawNet & CrimsonLogic Service Bureau, in person.

    The Magistrate's Court applies the simplified process (Order 65) by default; the District Court can too, by consent. The simplified process front-loads document production and limits interlocutory skirmishing.

      Send a letter of demand. The Rules require parties to genuinely consider amicable resolution before and during proceedings. Check the limitation period (6 years for most contract and tort claims). Some claim types (personal injury, medical negligence, non-injury motor accident) have a mandatory pre-action protocol.
      Letter of demandLimitation ActPre-action protocol (some claims)
    2. Pick the right court
      By value: ≤ S$60k Magistrate's; S$60k–S$250k District; above S$250k High Court. Filing in the wrong court risks transfer or costs penalties.
      Forum = nature + value
    3. Draft the Originating Claim and Statement of Claim
      The OC is the formal originating process; it is normally served together with a Statement of Claim setting out the facts and the relief sought. If the OC is only generally endorsed, the Statement of Claim must still follow within 14 days of service.

      Sealed OC issuedValid 3 months

      Personal service is required; you (or your employee) may serve it, or a lawyer, or in special cases a court process server. Take reasonable steps within 14 days (in Singapore) or 28 days (overseas, which first needs court approval to serve out). Then file a Memorandum of Service.

      The defendant files a Notice of Intention to Contest or Not Contest within 14 days of Statement of Claim service (in SG) or 21 days (out of SG).

      The court actively manages the case at case conferences (simplified case conferences in the MC). Parties exchange documents; the court may order affidavits of evidence-in-chief. Interlocutory applications are made by summons with a supporting affidavit, and are deliberately limited under the simplified process.
      Case conferenceSummons + affidavit (interlocutory)

      Witnesses give evidence (often by affidavit of evidence-in-chief) and are cross-examined. You make submissions. The court delivers judgment. You then draw up and file the order.

      Whether by default or on the merits, you now hold a judgment. If it is not honoured, proceed to enforcement.
      → Enforcement module

    Bull · best case

    Defendant does not respond. You file a memorandum of service, obtain default judgment, and the defendant pays without enforcement.

    Time: ~6–10 weeks   Cost: filing + service
    Base · likely

    Defendant contests; after pleadings and a case conference, the matter settles (often via an offer to settle) before trial.

    Time: 6–14 months   Cost: moderate, adverse-cost risk
    Bear · worst case

    Fully contested through trial, you lose or win less than claimed, face an adverse costs order, the loser appeals, and even a win needs hard enforcement.

    Time: 1–3 years   Cost: high + costs exposure

    Originating Application · when the facts are not really in dispute

    Used when you want the court to decide a point of law, construe a document, grant a statutory remedy, or make a declaration, and the material facts are largely undisputed (formerly the "Originating Summons"). It runs on affidavit evidence rather than a full trial, so it is usually faster.

    Either side can apply to convert an OA into an OC at any stage if it turns out the facts are genuinely contested.

    04

    Enforcement: a judgment is not the same as getting paid

    Winning is step one. If the other side will not comply, you enforce. Under the Rules of Court 2021 the language changed (you are the enforcement applicant, they are the enforcement respondent, the old "writ of execution" is an Enforcement Order, and the old "garnishee order" is an attachment of a debt), and crucially a single application can now seek several enforcement methods at once.

    1. Decide if enforcement is worth it
      Enforcement costs time and non-refundable fees with no guaranteed recovery. If the respondent has no assets, an Enforcement Order changes nothing. Assess willingness and ability to pay, and where assets sit, before spending more.
      No assets = no recovery
    2. Find the assets: Examination of the Enforcement Respondent
      If you do not know what the respondent owns, apply (summons without notice + affidavit) to examine them under oath about their assets and to produce documents. Searches at ACRA and property records help too.
      Application to examine (without notice) + affidavit
    3. Apply for the Enforcement Order
      File a single application (Form 38, Enforcement Order) selecting one or more methods. For a seizure-and-sale, the application can be made 3 days after the order is served on the respondent. The EO is valid for 12 months and can be extended.
      Enforcement Order · Form 38EO valid 12 monthsSingle app, multiple methods

    Seizure & Sale of property

    The Sheriff/Bailiff seizes the respondent's movable (or immovable) property and sells it to satisfy the debt. After seizure the respondent has 7 days to pay before sale. Property valued over S$20,000, or any immovable property, is sold by public auction. Some essentials are exempt; an HDB flat needs HDB approval.

    Form 38 + Notice of Seizure (Form 40)

    Attachment of a debt (ex-garnishee)

    Redirects money a third party owes the respondent (classically, the respondent's bank balance) to you. You must identify and prove the account/debt. Reaches bank deposits regardless of maturity.

    Form 38 (attachment) + Notice of Attachment

    Delivery / Possession of property

    Delivery: Sheriff seizes a specific movable item and delivers it to you. Possession: used to recover land/premises (for example, a landlord repossessing from a tenant), evicting an occupier who will not leave.

    Form 38 (delivery / possession)

    Committal (for breach of a court order)

    Where someone disobeys an order to do or stop doing an act, committal proceedings can lead to a fine or imprisonment. Procedurally heavy and a last resort; you must give an indemnity undertaking to the Sheriff/Police for costs.

    Committal application + statement + affidavit

    Sheriff attendance

    Requests for the Sheriff/Bailiff to attend at the place of execution are filed in the prescribed request form and carry scheduled fees.

    Request for Attendance of the Sheriff · Form B35

    Foreign judgments & enforcing abroad

    A foreign judgment may be registered here under the Reciprocal Enforcement of Foreign Judgments Act (gazetted jurisdictions) or sued on afresh at common law. If the respondent's assets are overseas, you may need to enforce a Singapore judgment in that country instead.

    REFJA registration / fresh action
    Bull

    Respondent has a known local bank account; attachment of debt recovers the full sum quickly.

    Base

    Mix of methods needed; partial recovery over several months after locating assets.

    Bear

    Assets hidden, dissipated, overseas, or non-existent; fees spent with little or nothing recovered.

    05

    Cross-cutting procedures (the situations that interrupt any track)

    These can arise inside almost any workflow above. In the app they live as reusable modules a user can be routed into mid-flow.

    Substituted service (you cannot serve in person)
    After at least two reasonable attempts at personal service, apply by summons supported by an affidavit. State the failed attempts, why personal service is impracticable, your proposed method, and why it will effectively bring the document to the defendant's notice. Methods include posting on the door/gate, email, or messaging where appropriate. Subject to court approval.
    Summons + affidavit
    Service out of Singapore
    Serving a defendant abroad needs the court's approval. File a supporting affidavit showing the court has jurisdiction or is the appropriate court (a good arguable case of sufficient nexus to Singapore), and the basis for service out. Timelines extend (for example, 5 weeks for a defence). Consider combining with an application to extend the originating process validity.
    Application for approval to serve out + affidavit
    Extending the validity of the originating process
    An OC is valid for 3 months. If you cannot serve in time (common with overseas or evasive defendants), apply before expiry to extend validity, seeking a period matched to how long service is realistically expected to take.
    Summons for extension of validity
    Amending a document already filed
    Amended pleadings must be endorsed showing they were amended, the date, and the authority (a court order or the specific Rule relied on). Electronic forms carry a standard amendment endorsement in the title (for example, "Amendment No. 2"). Some amendments need the court's permission.
    Endorsement required
    Compelling a witness (subpoena / summons to a witness)
    If a witness will not attend voluntarily, file a subpoena (Form 67, Rules of Court) via eLitigation; a self-represented person submits it at the Service Bureau. Collect the issued subpoena and serve it personally within the validity window. Urgent issuance close to trial costs more.
    Subpoena · Form 67
    Offer of amicable resolution / offer to settle
    The Rules require parties to genuinely consider amicable resolution throughout. A formal offer to settle carries costs consequences: refusing a reasonable offer and then doing worse at trial can flip costs against the refusing party. A powerful, cheap tool for a self-litigant to apply pressure.
    Offer to settleCosts consequences
    Setting aside a default judgment (if you are the defendant)
    A defendant served with a default judgment can apply to set it aside. A "regular" default judgment is usually set aside where the defendant shows a defence with real prospects of success, sometimes on conditions (such as paying money into court). An "irregular" one can be set aside as of right.
    Summons + affidavit (+ draft defence)
    Discontinuing or withdrawing
    You can discontinue a claim, but timing affects costs: discontinue late and you may pay the other side's costs to date. In the SCT you file a Withdrawal Request in CJTS, subject to approval, and filing fees are not refunded.
    Notice of discontinuance / Withdrawal Request
    Transfer between courts
    A case filed in the wrong court (or that outgrows it) can be transferred. Parties can also consent for the District Court to hear a claim above its normal limit, or to cap a claim to fit a lower court. Filing in the wrong forum can attract costs penalties.
    Application / consent to transfer
    Appeals
    From the State Courts, appeals generally go to the General Division of the High Court. From the SCT, an appeal lies to a District Judge (and onward only with permission). From the High Court, appeals lie to the Appellate Division or, for prescribed categories, the Court of Appeal. A right of appeal is not automatic; some need permission. Strict deadlines apply, so check them immediately after judgment.
    Tight deadlinesPermission may be required
